How I Stopped Overthinking at 2AM (And Finally Found Peace)

Stories · 2026-10-24

My brain is running a marathon it never trained for.

It’s 2AM. My room is dark. My tea is cold. And the questions keep coming:

Why did I say that today?

Does Zoe think I was rude?

What if I fail tomorrow?

What if I never figure it out?

You know that voice, right? The one that gets louder when the world gets quiet.

The night I made my thoughts tea

For years, I thought overthinking meant something was wrong with me. That I was broken. That I had to fix my brain.

I tried telling myself, just don’t think about it.

It never worked. You can’t tell a storm to stop raining.

Then one night, at 2:13 AM, I tried something different.

Instead of fighting my thoughts, I made them tea.

I know. It sounds silly. But I got up, took my yellow mug with the little pink hearts, and said out loud:

“Okay, brain. I see you. You’re scared. Let’s talk.”

I didn’t argue. I didn’t judge. I let my thoughts sit next to me like a friend who showed up uninvited but clearly needed company.

It was awkward at first. My thoughts talked over each other, and twice I reached for my phone just to escape them. But I stayed for one cup of tea. That was all I asked of myself.

Paper, not phone

Then I wrote every thought down. On paper, not on my phone.

I’m scared I said something stupid today.

I’m scared people don’t like the real me.

I’m scared my future won’t get better.

On paper, they looked smaller. Less like monsters, more like worries. Just human worries.

I remembered something then.

When I was little, I woke up scared one night, and Mom came in. She didn’t say “stop worrying.” She didn’t say anything. She just sat on the edge of my bed until my breathing slowed.

I had forgotten that. Maybe that’s what I was trying to do with the tea. Sit on the edge of my own bed.

The text from Zoe

The next night, my phone buzzed. It was Zoe, my best friend, the one with a pencil always tucked into her curls and a tiny gold star pinned to every cardigan.

“Can’t sleep again. I keep thinking everyone secretly finds me annoying. Ugh. Tell me I’m being dramatic.”

I didn’t send her an argument. I typed:

“You’re not annoying. You’re tired. You’ve been carrying a lot. Come over, I’ll make tea.”

I hit send. Then I sat very still.

I had never talked to myself like that. Not once.

The three questions

That’s when I came up with the three questions I now ask every night. I wrote them on a card and tucked it inside my notebook:

  1. Is this true, or is this my fear talking?Most of the time, it’s fear. Not fact.
  2. Can I do something about this right now?If yes, I write one tiny step for tomorrow. If no, I tell my brain: “We’ll handle this tomorrow at 10AM. Not now.”
  3. What would I say to Zoe if she told me this?I’d never call her stupid. I’d say: “You’re tired. You’re doing your best. Let’s sleep.”

· · ·

The night it didn’t work

I’d like to tell you it worked perfectly.

It didn’t.

Two weeks later, I sat up at 3AM with the tea, the paper, and the three questions. And nothing worked.

The tea had gone cold. The page stayed blank. Every answer I wrote sounded like a lie, and the clock kept glowing: 3:07, then 3:12, then 3:19.

I felt like a fraud. All those calm nights, and here I was, right back at the beginning. I almost threw the notebook across the room.

Instead, I texted Zoe: “It’s not working tonight.”

She wrote back in seconds: “It doesn’t have to work every night. Some nights, you just sit with it. That counts too.”

So I did. I didn’t fix anything. I didn’t answer a single question. I just sat there, next to my thoughts, the way Mom once sat next to me.

Somewhere near 4AM, the window turned from black to deep blue. Eventually, I slept.

In the morning, the mug was still on the nightstand, the tea cold, the notebook open to a blank page. I looked at it for a while and decided it could stay there.

My night routine now

  • A mug of warm tea, and no phone for 30 minutes before bed
  • Paper on the nightstand: dump every thought, no filter
  • The three questions, then close the notebook and say: “We’re done for tonight.”

It isn’t perfect. Some nights I still overthink. But now I have a place to put my thoughts, instead of carrying them in my chest.

· · ·

If you’re reading this at 2AM with a busy brain:

You’re a person with a big heart and a mind that cares a little too much.

Make it some tea. Write it down. Tell a friend if you can.

And let yourself rest.

With love,
Lina
