How I Stopped Replaying Conversations at Night

Stories · 2026-10-27

At 11:48 PM, I pressed play on a conversation that had ended nine hours earlier.

It was a tiny one. At lunch, Dev had shown me his drawing of a lighthouse, and I had said, “Oh. It’s… interesting.” He smiled with only half of his face and said, “Yeah. Thanks.” Then he put the drawing back in his folder and went quiet.

That was all. Eleven seconds, maybe.

But in the dark, my mind loaded it like a movie and hit play. Interesting. His half smile. The folder closing. Play.

Why did I say interesting?

Did he hear it as an insult?

What if I had said beautiful instead?

The director’s cut

Here is the strange part. I didn’t just replay it. I edited it.

In take two, I said, “Wow, the lighthouse is great!” In take three, I said it with a bigger smile. In take five, I gave a whole speech about the shading, which I had not noticed at all. By take nine, I had written Dev a heartfelt apology and an award.

None of those takes were real. The only real take was the first one, and I couldn’t change it, no matter how many times I rewound.

By 1:15 AM, I had watched it fourteen times. My stomach felt like I had eaten a whole bag of paperclips. And the weirdest thing was that the movie had not gotten any better. Replaying it didn’t fix a single frame. It just made me tired.

That’s when I noticed something. A replay never ends with a decision. It ends with another replay.

· · ·

Fourteen takes

The next day, I told Zoe everything. She listened with a sandwich in one hand and her pencil, as always, tucked in her curls.

“Wait,” she said. “How many times did you watch it?”

“Fourteen.”

She counted on her fingers, slowly, as if she needed the evidence. “And did the movie change?”

“…No.”

“Then you’re not studying it, Lina. You’re just rewatching it, hoping the ending changes.”

I opened my mouth to argue, and then I closed it, because she was right. She gave me a tiny, kind shrug. “One replay is a lesson. Fourteen is a habit.”

So we made a rule, right there on the steps, with crumbs on our knees. I would get one replay. Just one. And when it finished, I had to write two short sentences on paper: What did actually happen? and What is one small thing I can do tomorrow?

Not a speech. Not an apology. Just one small thing.

What Dev was thinking

That afternoon, I walked up to Dev at the lockers. My knees were doing a strange little dance.

“Hey,” I said. “About your lighthouse. I said ‘interesting’ yesterday, and I think it came out weird. I really like the sky. The part with the little stars.”

Dev blinked. “Oh! You did? I wasn’t even…” He laughed, a little embarrassed. “Honestly, I was thinking about my dentist appointment. I went quiet because I had to leave early. I didn’t notice you said anything weird.”

“So you weren’t upset?”

“Lina. I was thinking about a tooth.”

Evidence, again, in the daylight. I had been rewinding a movie in which I was the only person who cared about the plot.

Then he held up his folder and said, “Do you want to see the other one? I drew a second lighthouse.” And we looked at it together, and it really was interesting, in the best way.

Roll credits

I’d love to tell you the replays stopped forever. They didn’t.

Three weeks later, I said something silly during a group presentation and heard it again at 11:32 PM, loud and clear, with a close-up of my own face. The projector in my head clicked on. I felt it warming up.

But this time I took out my notebook. I let it play once, all the way through. Then I wrote my two sentences. What happened: I made a joke and two people laughed. One small thing: nothing. It was fine.

And then, because the movie wanted to start again, I did something new. I took a sticky note, wrote Roll credits on it, and pressed it on my lamp.

That’s it. That’s the whole trick. When the movie tries to start over, I look at the lamp and think, I already watched that one. The credits are rolling.

Sometimes it works right away. Sometimes I have to say it three times, like asking a very persistent cat to get off the keyboard. But the movie doesn’t get to play fourteen times anymore.

These days, my notebook has a page called Takes, and most of the entries are only two lines long. One of them says, Dev was thinking about a tooth. It’s my favorite.

I still replay conversations sometimes. I just stop at the credits now.

With love,
Lina
