The Sunday Night Dread Is Not a Prophecy

Stories · 2026-10-28

The dread arrives every Sunday at exactly 6:47 PM, as if it has a bus schedule.

It doesn’t come with a reason. The weekend is still warm, there is still half a pizza in the fridge, and nothing terrible has happened. Yet at 6:47, a gray feeling settles into my stomach and whispers, Tomorrow is going to be awful.

I don’t know what is awful about it. I only know that the feeling is very sure, and it sounds like a weather forecast delivered by someone who has never been wrong.

What if I forgot something for school?

What if Monday goes badly, the whole week goes badly, and I can’t stop it?

Why can’t I enjoy my Sunday?

A forecast is not a prophecy

When I told Zoe, she did not say it was silly. She said, “Oh, I know that one. The Sunday Gray.”

“You get it too?”

“Every week. Mine arrives at five and hangs around until about nine.” She shrugged, the way you shrug when you have lived with a roommate for a long time. “Here’s what I figured out. It’s a forecast. It says cloudy tomorrow. But a forecast isn’t the day. Some days it says storm and I get a bright afternoon.”

I thought about that. The dread was not telling me about Monday. It was telling me about Sunday, about being tired from the weekend and a little sad it was ending. It had borrowed Monday’s name, that was all.

· · ·

The Sunday soft landing

“Okay,” said Zoe. “We don’t fight it. We make a soft landing.”

We wrote the plan on the back of a menu at the bakery, and I kept it afterward.

  1. Pack it now. Bag by the door, clothes on the chair. Fewer surprises for Monday me.
  2. Name the forecast. “This is the Sunday Gray. It is a feeling, not a fact.”
  3. Plant one good thing. Something small to look forward to on Monday morning.

The third one was my favorite. We decided that a good thing did not need to be big. A cinnamon bun. A song I love on the walk to school. A sticker in my notebook that I will find at lunch.

The little star in my pocket

The next Sunday, at 6:47, the gray feeling came right on time. I almost laughed. I said to it, quietly, “Hi, Sunday Gray. I know. You’re a forecast.”

It didn’t go away. But when I did not fight it, it stayed a little smaller. I packed my bag, laid out my clothes, and wrote one thing on a small paper star: cinnamon bun at the bakery. I slid it into the front pocket of my backpack.

On Monday morning, I found the star, and I remembered, and the day started with a small, unexpected smile. Monday was not great. It was not terrible either. It was a normal day, with a math quiz in it and the best cinnamon bun in the world.

The gray still visits. But I don’t think of it as a prophecy anymore. I think of it as the Sunday weather. It rolls in, it rolls out, and tomorrow is its own day entirely.

With love,
Lina
